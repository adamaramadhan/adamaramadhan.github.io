I am Adam Ramadhan, a PhD candidate at Universiteit Leiden working on the social and intellectual history of early Shiʿi Islam as part of the ERC Starting Grant funded project Embodied Imamate: Mapping the Development of the Early Shiʿi Community 700-900 CE.

I first read for a BA in Arabic and Islamic Studies at the University of Leeds, graduating with first-class honours and several prizes. I then spent several years at the Shiʿi seminary (ḥawza) at Al-Mahdi Institute where I was trained in the deep and critical reading of classical Arabic texts across a range of disciplines. During this time, I also completed an MSt in Islamic Studies and History at the University of Oxford, graduating with a distinction.

Prior to my move to Leiden, I worked in academic administration and as the Head Librarian of a specialist Islamic studies library. I co-founded and currently the co-chair the Shii Studies Network. I am also the Graduate Student Representative on the board of Middle East Medievalists. I was formerly the Coordinator of the British Association for Islamic Studies Prize in the Study of Islam and the Muslim World.

As part of the Embodied Imamate project, I work on the demarcation of communal boundaries within the early Imāmī community. I am interested in how the Imāms and the Imāmī community utilised legal mechanisms to regulate social inclusion and exclusion in the process of forming a distinct community. Alongside this, I work on prosopographical material to build a clearer picture of what the networks of associates of the Shiʿi Imāms may have looked like, and how the diversity of such networks informs our understanding of interactions across communal boundaries in early Islam.

Aside from my doctoral research, I maintain an interest in Islamic legal studies more broadly and am working on several publications in this regard. I also have an interest in the social, cultural, and intellectual history of the Persian Gulf, in particular the history of Bahrain.
